First, the trains. I think most people probably think the same as we do. In Japan, the trains and subways are fast, clean, and almost always on time. They can take you almost anywhere, so we didn’t need a car or a taxi. That’s also why the streets in Japan aren’t full of scooters like in Taiwan. With an IC card and Google Maps, getting around was very easy. This makes traveling on your own, or even backpacking, much easier in Japan.

---
Second, Japan is very clean and quiet. When I first visited Japan as a kid, I was shocked that there were almost no trash cans in public places, but there was still no trash on the ground. In Taiwan, we can easily find one on the street or in a convenience store. In Japan, people don’t talk loudly, whether they’re in shops, on trains, or on the street. Everyone tries not to bother others, and we really liked that.

---
Third, we loved the atmosphere at night. The city lights are beautiful, and it feels safe to walk around, even late at night. We didn't need a plan. Just walking around after dinner and buying a drink from a vending machine already felt special.

---
But not everything was perfect. Famous places were really crowded, so it was hard to take photos or enjoy the view. In some big cities, things felt quite expensive. And in some situations, the language gap became a barrier for us. For example, ordering food or asking for directions was a little difficult, but after all we still had a great time in Japan.
