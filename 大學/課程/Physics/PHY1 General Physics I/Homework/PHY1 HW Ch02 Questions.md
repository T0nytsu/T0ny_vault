## Textbook Examples

### EUP Example 2.1
*Speed and Velocity: Flying with a Connection*

To get a cheap flight from Houston to Kansas City—a distance of $1000\ \mathrm{km}$—you have to connect in Minneapolis, $700\ \mathrm{km}$ north of Kansas City. The flight to Minneapolis takes $2.2\ \mathrm{h}$, then you have a $30$-min layover, and then a $1.3$-h flight to Kansas City. What are your average velocity and your average speed on this trip?

### EUP Example 2.2
*Instantaneous Velocity: A Rocket Ascends*

The altitude of a rocket in the first half-minute of its ascent is given by $x = bt^{2}$, where the constant $b$ is $2.90\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find a general expression for the rocket's velocity as a function of time and from it the instantaneous velocity at $t = 20\ \mathrm{s}$. Also find an expression for the average velocity, and compare your two velocity expressions.

### EUP Conceptual Example 2.1
*Acceleration without Velocity?*

Can an object be accelerating even though it's not moving?

**Making the Connection** Just $0.010\ \mathrm{s}$ before it peaks, the ball in Fig. 2.8 is moving upward at $0.098\ \mathrm{m/s}$; $0.010\ \mathrm{s}$ after it peaks, it's moving downward with the same speed. What's its average acceleration over this $0.02$-s interval?

### EUP Example 2.3
*Motion with Constant Acceleration: Landing a Jetliner*

A jetliner touches down at $270\ \mathrm{km/h}$. The plane then decelerates (i.e., undergoes acceleration directed opposite its velocity) at $4.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. What's the minimum runway length on which this aircraft can land?

### EUP Example 2.4
*Motion with Two Objects: Speed Trap!*

A speeding motorist zooms through a $50\ \mathrm{km/h}$ zone at $75\ \mathrm{km/h}$ (that's $21\ \mathrm{m/s}$) without noticing a stationary police car. The police officer immediately heads after the speeder, accelerating at $2.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. When the officer catches up to the speeder, how far down the road are they, and how fast is the police car going?

### EUP Example 2.5
*Constant Acceleration Due to Gravity: Cliff Diving*

A diver drops from a $10$-m-high cliff. At what speed does he enter the water, and how long is he in the air?

### EUP Example 2.6
*Constant Acceleration Due to Gravity: Tossing a Ball*

You toss a ball straight up at $7.3\ \mathrm{m/s}$; it leaves your hand at $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ above the floor. Find when it hits the floor, the maximum height it reaches, and its speed when it passes your hand on the way down.

## Average Velocity and Average Speed

### EUP 2.13
Starting from home, you bicycle $24\ \mathrm{km}$ north in $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$ and then turn around and pedal straight home in $1.5\ \mathrm{h}$. What are your (a) displacement at the end of the first $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$, (b) average velocity over the first $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$, (c) average velocity for the homeward leg of the trip, (d) displacement for the entire trip, and (e) average velocity for the entire trip?

### EUP 2.52
A plane leaves London for Singapore, $10{,}886\ \mathrm{km}$ away. With a strong tailwind, its speed is $1040\ \mathrm{km/h}$. At the same time, a second plane leaves Singapore for London. Flying into the wind, it makes only $765\ \mathrm{km/h}$. When and where do the two planes pass each other?

### EUP 2.83
Consider an object traversing a distance $L$, part of the way at speed $v_{1}$ and the rest of the way at speed $v_{2}$. Find expressions for the object's average speed over the entire distance $L$ when the object moves at each of the two speeds $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ for (a) half the total *time* and (b) half the total *distance*. (c) In which case is the average speed greater?

## Velocity from Position

### EUP 2.53
An object's position is given by $x = bt + ct^{3}$, where $b = 1.50\ \mathrm{m/s}$, $c = 0.640\ \mathrm{m/s^3}$, and $t$ is time in seconds. To study the limiting process leading to the instantaneous velocity, calculate the object's average velocity over time intervals from (a) $1.00\ \mathrm{s}$ to $3.00\ \mathrm{s}$, (b) $1.50\ \mathrm{s}$ to $2.50\ \mathrm{s}$, and (c) $1.95\ \mathrm{s}$ to $2.05\ \mathrm{s}$. (d) Find the instantaneous velocity as a function of time by differentiating, and compare its value at $2\ \mathrm{s}$ with your average velocities.

### EUP 2.84
An object's position as a function of time is given by $x = bt^{2} - ct^{4}$, where $b$ has the value $1.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, which puts the object at $x = 0$ at $t = 0$. (a) Find the value of $c$ such that the object will again be at $x = 0$ when $t = 2.54\ \mathrm{s}$. Also, find (b) the object's speed and (c) its acceleration at that time.

## Average Acceleration

### EUP 2.21
Starting from rest, a subway train first accelerates to $25\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and then brakes. Forty-eight seconds after starting, it's moving at $18\ \mathrm{m/s}$. What's its average acceleration in this $48$-s interval?

## Constant Acceleration with One Object

### EUP 2.29
A rocket starts from rest and rises with constant acceleration to a height $h$, at which point it's rising at speed $v$. Find expressions for (a) the rocket's acceleration and (b) the time it takes to reach height $h$.

### EUP 2.62
A particle leaves its initial position $x_{0}$ at time $t = 0$, moving in the positive $x$-direction with speed $v_{0}$ but undergoing acceleration of magnitude $a$ in the negative $x$-direction. Find expressions for (a) the time when it returns to $x_{0}$ and (b) its speed when it passes that point.

### EUP 2.66
A motorist suddenly notices a stalled car and slams on the brakes, slowing at $6.3\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Unfortunately, this isn't enough, and a collision ensues. From the damage sustained, police estimate that the car was going $18\ \mathrm{km/h}$ at the time of the collision. They also measure skid marks $34\ \mathrm{m}$ long. (a) How fast was the motorist going when the brakes were first applied? (b) How much time elapsed from the initial braking to the collision?

### EUP 2.67
A racing car undergoing constant acceleration covers $140\ \mathrm{m}$ in $3.6\ \mathrm{s}$. (a) If it's moving at $53\ \mathrm{m/s}$ at the end of this interval, what was its speed at the beginning of the interval? (b) How far did it travel from rest to the end of the $140$-m distance?

## Constant Acceleration with Two Objects

### EUP 2.55
In a $400$-m drag race, two cars start at the same time, and each maintains a constant acceleration. The winner's acceleration is $4.25\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the winner reaches the finish line $248\ \mathrm{ms}$ before the loser does. By what distance is the loser behind when the winner reaches the finish line?

### EUP 2.68
The maximum braking acceleration of a car on a dry road is about $8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. If two cars move head-on toward each other at $88\ \mathrm{km/h}$ ($55\ \mathrm{mi/h}$), and their drivers brake when they're $85\ \mathrm{m}$ apart, will they collide? If so, at what relative speed? If not, how far apart will they be when they stop? Plot distance versus time for both cars on a single graph.

### EUP 2.70
You're speeding at $85\ \mathrm{km/h}$ when you notice that you're only $10\ \mathrm{m}$ behind the car in front of you, which is moving at the legal speed limit of $60\ \mathrm{km/h}$. You slam on your brakes, and your car slows at the rate of $4.2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Assuming the other car continues at constant speed, will you collide? If so, at what relative speed? If not, what will be the distance between the cars at their closest approach?

## Free Fall with One Object

### EUP 2.37
A model rocket leaves the ground, heading straight up with speed $v$. Find expressions for (a) its maximum altitude and (b) its speed when it's at half the maximum altitude.

### EUP 2.59
A fireworks rocket explodes at a height of $82.0\ \mathrm{m}$, producing fragments with velocities ranging from $7.68\ \mathrm{m/s}$ downward to $16.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$ upward. Over what time interval are fragments hitting the ground?

### EUP 2.73
A falling object travels one-fourth of its total distance in the last second of its fall. From what height was it dropped?

### EUP 2.85
Ice skaters, ballet dancers, and basketball players executing vertical leaps often give the illusion of "hanging" almost motionless near the top of the leap. To see why this is, consider a leap to maximum height $h$. Of the total time spent in the air, what fraction is spent in the upper half (i.e., at $y > \tfrac{1}{2}h$)?

### EUP 2.86
You're staring idly out your dorm window when you see a water balloon fall past. If the balloon takes $0.22\ \mathrm{s}$ to cross the $1.3$-m vertical extent of the window, from what height above the window was it dropped?

### EUP 2.90
(a) For the ball in Example 2.6, find its velocity just before it hits the floor. (b) Suppose you had tossed a second ball straight down at $7.3\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (from the same place $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ above the floor). What would its velocity be just before it hits the floor? (c) When would the second ball hit the floor? (Interpret any multiple answers.)

### EUP 2.91
Your roommate is an aspiring novelist and asks your opinion on a matter of physics. The novel's central character is kept awake at night by a leaky faucet. The sink is $19.6\ \mathrm{cm}$ below the faucet. At the instant one drop leaves the faucet, another strikes the sink below and two more are in between on the way down. How many drops per second are keeping the protagonist awake?

## Free Fall with Two Objects

### EUP 2.77
Two divers jump from a $3.00$-m platform. One jumps upward at $1.80\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and the second steps off the platform as the first passes it on the way down. (a) What are their speeds as they hit the water? (b) Which hits the water first and by how much?

### EUP 2.78
A balloon is rising at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ when its passenger throws a ball straight up at $12\ \mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the balloon. How much later does the passenger catch the ball?

### EUP 2.97
A ball is dropped from rest at a height $h_{0}$ above the ground. At the same instant, a second ball is launched with speed $v_{0}$ straight up from the ground, at a point directly below where the other ball is dropped. (a) Find a condition on $v_{0}$ such that the two balls will collide in mid-air. (b) Find an expression for the height at which they collide.

## Nonconstant Acceleration

### EUP 2.88
An object starts moving in a straight line from position $x_{0}$, at time $t = 0$, with velocity $v_{0}$. Its acceleration is given by $a = a_{0} + bt$, where $a_{0}$ and $b$ are constants. Use integration to find expressions for (a) the instantaneous velocity and (b) the position, as functions of time.

### EUP 2.94
An object's acceleration increases quadratically with time: $a(t) = bt^{2}$, where $b = 0.041\ \mathrm{m/s^4}$. If the object starts from rest, how far does it travel in $6.3\ \mathrm{s}$?

### EUP 2.95
An object's velocity as a function of time is given by $v(t) = bt - ct^{3}$, where $b$ and $c$ are positive constants with appropriate units. If the object starts at $x = 0$ at time $t = 0$, find expressions for (a) the time when it's again at $x = 0$ and (b) its acceleration at that time.

### EUP 2.96
An object's acceleration decreases exponentially with time: $a(t) = a_{0}e^{-bt}$, where $a_{0}$ and $b$ are constants. (a) Assuming the object starts from rest, determine its velocity as a function of time. (b) Will its speed increase indefinitely? (c) Will it travel indefinitely far from its starting point?
