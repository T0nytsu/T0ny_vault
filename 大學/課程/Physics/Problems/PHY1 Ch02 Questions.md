## EUP §2.1 Average Motion

### EUP 2.13
Starting from home, you bicycle $24\ \mathrm{km}$ north in $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$, then turn around and ride straight home in $1.5\ \mathrm{h}$. Find
- (a) your displacement at the end of the first $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$,
- (b) your average velocity over the first $2.5\ \mathrm{h}$,
- (c) your average velocity on the homeward leg,
- (d) your displacement for the entire trip,
- (e) your average velocity for the entire trip.

## EUP §2.3 Acceleration

### EUP 2.21
A subway train starts from rest, accelerates to $25\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and then brakes. Forty-eight seconds after starting it is moving at $18\ \mathrm{m/s}$. What is its average acceleration in this $48$-s interval?

## EUP Chapter 2 Problems

### EUP 2.52
A plane leaves London for Singapore, $10\,886\ \mathrm{km}$ away, flying at $1040\ \mathrm{km/h}$. At the same time a second plane leaves Singapore for London at $765\ \mathrm{km/h}$. When and where do the two planes pass each other?

### EUP 2.53
An object's position is $x = bt + ct^{3}$, where $b = 1.50\ \mathrm{m/s}$, $c = 0.640\ \mathrm{m/s^3}$, and $t$ is the time in seconds. Calculate the average velocity over the time intervals from
- (a) $1.00\ \mathrm{s}$ to $3.00\ \mathrm{s}$,
- (b) $1.50\ \mathrm{s}$ to $2.50\ \mathrm{s}$,
- (c) $1.95\ \mathrm{s}$ to $2.05\ \mathrm{s}$.
- (d) Find the instantaneous velocity as a function of time by differentiating, and compare its value at $2\ \mathrm{s}$ with the average velocities.

### EUP 2.55
In a $400$-m drag race two cars start at the same time, each with constant acceleration. The winner's acceleration is $4.25\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the winner reaches the finish line $248\ \mathrm{ms}$ before the loser. How far behind is the loser when the winner finishes?

### EUP 2.58
The position of a car in a drag race is measured each second.

| Time $t$ (s) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Position $x$ (m) | 0 | 1.7 | 6.2 | 17 | 24 | 40 |

Assume the acceleration is approximately constant. Plot position against a quantity that should make the graph a straight line, fit a line to the data, and determine the approximate acceleration.

### EUP 2.59
A fireworks rocket explodes at a height of $82.0\ \mathrm{m}$. The fragments have velocities ranging from $7.68\ \mathrm{m/s}$ downward to $16.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$ upward. Over what time interval do fragments hit the ground?

### EUP 2.66
A motorist notices a stalled car and brakes, slowing at $6.3\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, but hits the stalled car at $18\ \mathrm{km/h}$. The skid marks are $34\ \mathrm{m}$ long.
- (a) How fast was the motorist going when the brakes were first applied?
- (b) How much time elapsed from the start of braking to the collision?

### EUP 2.68
The maximum braking acceleration of a car on a dry road is about $8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Two cars move head-on toward each other at $88\ \mathrm{km/h}$, and both drivers brake when the cars are $85\ \mathrm{m}$ apart. Will they collide? If so, at what relative speed? If not, how far apart are they when they stop? Plot distance versus time for both cars on one graph.

### EUP 2.70
You are driving at $85\ \mathrm{km/h}$, only $10\ \mathrm{m}$ behind a car moving at $60\ \mathrm{km/h}$. You brake, slowing at $4.2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, while the other car keeps its speed. Will you collide? If so, at what relative speed? If not, what is the distance between the cars at their closest approach?

### EUP 2.73
CH. A falling object travels one-fourth of its total distance in the last second of its fall. From what height was it dropped?

### EUP 2.77
CH. Two divers jump from a $3.00$-m platform. One jumps upward at $1.80\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and the second steps off the platform as the first passes it on the way down.
- (a) What are their speeds as they hit the water?
- (b) Which hits the water first, and by how much?

### EUP 2.78
A balloon is rising at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ when its passenger throws a ball straight up at $12\ \mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the balloon. How much later does the passenger catch the ball?

### EUP 2.83
An object covers a distance $L$, part of the way at speed $v_{1}$ and the rest at speed $v_{2}$. Find expressions for its average speed over the whole distance when it moves at each of the two speeds for
- (a) half the total time,
- (b) half the total distance.
- (c) In which case is the average speed greater?

### EUP 2.85
CH. Consider a vertical leap to maximum height $h$. Of the total time spent in the air, what fraction is spent in the upper half, that is, at $y > \tfrac{1}{2}h$?

### EUP 2.86
A water balloon falls past a window. It takes $0.22\ \mathrm{s}$ to cross the $1.3$-m vertical extent of the window. From what height above the window was it dropped?

### EUP 2.88
CH. An object starts moving in a straight line from position $x_{0}$ at time $t = 0$, with velocity $v_{0}$. Its acceleration is $a = a_{0} + bt$, where $a_{0}$ and $b$ are constants. Use integration to find expressions for
- (a) the instantaneous velocity,
- (b) the position,

as functions of time.

### EUP 2.91
A faucet leaks drops into a sink $19.6\ \mathrm{cm}$ below it. At the instant one drop leaves the faucet, another strikes the sink, and two more are in between on the way down. How many drops fall per second?

### EUP 2.95
CH. An object's velocity is $v(t) = bt - ct^{3}$, where $b$ and $c$ are positive constants. The object starts at $x = 0$ at time $t = 0$. Find expressions for
- (a) the time when it is again at $x = 0$,
- (b) its acceleration at that time.

### EUP 2.96
CH. An object's acceleration decreases exponentially with time: $a(t) = a_{0}e^{-bt}$, where $a_{0}$ and $b$ are constants.
- (a) The object starts from rest. Determine its velocity as a function of time.
- (b) Will its speed increase indefinitely?
- (c) Will it travel indefinitely far from its starting point?

### EUP 2.97
CH. A ball is dropped from rest at a height $h_{0}$ above the ground. At the same instant a second ball is launched straight up from the ground with speed $v_{0}$, directly below the first.
- (a) Find a condition on $v_{0}$ such that the two balls collide in mid-air.
- (b) Find an expression for the height at which they collide.
