## Row Reduction by Row-Elementary Matrices

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\quad A \overset{r}{\sim} H \\
&E_{k} \cdots E_{2}E_{1}A = H.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!example] Example
> Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 4 & 5 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$
> Find a matrix $C$ such that
> $CA$ is a matrix in row-echelon form.

> [!solution]- Solution
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 4 & 5 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \overset{R_{12}}{\sim} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 4 & 5 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \\[6pt]
> &\overset{R_{13}^{-2}}{\sim} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \overset{R_{23}^{1}}{\sim} \underset{\substack{\shortparallel \\ \textstyle H}}{\underline{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & -3 \end{bmatrix}}} \\[6pt]
> &\implies \bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{R_{23}^{1}R_{13}^{-2}R_{12}}\,A = H
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> $$
> r_{23}^{1}\bigl(\,\underline{r_{13}^{-2}}\bigl(\,\underline{r_{12}(I)}\,\bigr)\bigr)
> $$
> $$
> C = \underset{\textstyle R_{23}^{1}}{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}\underset{\textstyle R_{13}^{-2}}{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}\underset{\textstyle R_{12}}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}

## Row-Equivalent Systems

> [!theorem] Theorem 1.6
> $A, H : m \times n$, $b, c : m \times 1$.
> If $[A \mid b] \overset{r}{\sim} [H \mid c]$
> then the linear systems $Ax = b$ and $Hx = c$
> have the same solution sets.

> [!proof]- Proof
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\because [A \mid b] \overset{r}{\sim} [H \mid c] \\
> &\therefore \exists\, P : \text{invertible s.t.} \\
> &\qquad P[A \mid b] = [H \mid c] \\
> &\implies [\underline{PA} \mid Pb] = [H \mid c] \\
> &\implies PA = H,\ Pb = c
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\qquad x \text{ is a solution of } Ax = b \\
> &\overset{P\,:\,\text{invertible}}{\iff} \underline{PA}x = Pb \\
> &\iff Hx = c \\
> &\iff x \text{ is a solution of } Hx = c \qquad \blacksquare
> \end{aligned}
> $$

> [!example] Example
> Solve the following linear systems:
> $$
> \text{(a)}\ \left\{\begin{array}{r} x_2 - 3x_3 = -5 \\ 2x_1 + 3x_2 - x_3 = 7 \\ 4x_1 + 5x_2 - 2x_3 = 10 \end{array}\right. \qquad \text{(b)}\ \left\{\begin{array}{r} x_1 - 3x_2 + x_3 = 1 \\ 2x_1 - x_2 - 2x_3 = 2 \\ x_1 + 2x_2 - 3x_3 = -1 \end{array}\right. \qquad \text{(c)}\ \left\{\begin{array}{r} x_2 - x_3 = 0 \\ x_1 \hphantom{{} + 3x_2} - 3x_3 = -1 \\ -x_1 + 3x_2 \hphantom{{} - 3x_3} = 1 \end{array}\right.

## Consistent and Inconsistent Systems

> [!definition] Definition
> A linear system has one or more solutions, it is said to be consistent.
> Otherwise, it is called inconsistent.

> [!theorem] Theorem 1.7
> Let $Ax = b$ be a linear system
> Let $[A \mid b] \overset{r}{\sim} [H \mid \underline{c}]$, where $H$ is in row-echelon form.
> Then (1) $Ax = b$ is $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{inconsistent}}$ iff $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{[H \mid c]}$ has a row
> with $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{all entries } 0}$ to the
> left of the partition and
> a $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{nonzero}}$ entry to the $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{right}}$
> of the partition.
> (2). If $Ax = b$ is consistent and every column in $H$
> contains a pivot, then the system has $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{a unique solution}}$.
> (3) If $Ax = b$ is consistent and some $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{column of } H}$ $\bbox[border-bottom:2px solid rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))]{\text{has no pivot}}$
> the system has infinitely many solutions.

> [!proof]- Proof
> Let $A : m \times n$.
> (1) **(⇐)** If $[H \mid c]$ has an $i$th row with all entries $0$ to the
> left of the partition and a nonzero entry $c_i$ to the
> right of the partition,
> $$
> \Bigl(\ \underset{\substack{\shortparallel \\ \textstyle 0}}{0x_1 + 0x_2 + \cdots + 0x_n} = \underset{\substack{\nshortparallel \\ \textstyle 0}}{c_i}\ \Bigr).
> $$
> which has no solutions.
> Hence, by Thm 1.6, $Ax = b$ has no solution.
>
> **(⇒)** Assume that $[H \mid c]$ has no row with
> all entries $0$ to the left of the partition
> and a nonzero entry to the right of the partition.
> If $[H \mid c]$ has a zero row vector $(0x_1 + 0x_2 + \cdots + 0x_n = 0)$
> we can remove the row from the system.
> Repeat this process, we can obtain
> a new system $[H \mid c]$ with no zero row vectors.
> (a) If every column has a pivot, the value of
> each $x_j$ is uniquely determined.
> (b) For each $j$th column that has no pivot,
> we can set $x_j$ as a variable (any value) and
> solve the other $x_i$ by back substitution. $\blacksquare$

> [!note] Note
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\quad Ax = b \text{ is consistent} \\
> &\iff \exists\, x = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} \text{ s.t. } Ax = b \\
> &\iff x_1A^{(1)} + x_2A^{(2)} + \cdots + x_nA^{(n)} \\
> &\hphantom{\iff x_1A^{(1)} + x_2A^{(2)}} = b \quad \textcolor{rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))}{\text{columns of } A} \\
> &\iff b \in \operatorname{span}\bigl(A^{(1)}, A^{(2)}, \cdots A^{(n)}\bigr)
> \end{aligned}
> $$

> [!example] Example
> Determine whether the vector $b = \underline{[1, -7, -4]}$
> is the span of the vectors $v = \underline{[2, 1, 1]}$ and
> $w = \underline{[1, 3, 2]}$.

> [!solution]- Solution
> $$
> \text{Let } A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix},\ b = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -7 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}
> $$
> $$
> \underline{[A \mid b]} = \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 & -7 \\ 1 & 2 & -4 \end{array}\right] \overset{r}{\sim} \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & -7 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -4 \end{array}\right]\!\begin{matrix} \vphantom{0} \\ \hookleftarrow{\times}(-2) \\ \hookleftarrow{\times}(-1) \end{matrix}
> $$
> $$
> \overset{r}{\sim} \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & -7 \\ 0 & -5 & 15 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 \end{array}\right] \overset{r}{\sim} \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} \textcolor{rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))}{\enclose{circle}{\textcolor{var(--text-normal)}{1}}} & 3 & -7 \\ 0 & \textcolor{rgb(var(--color-yellow-rgb))}{\enclose{circle}{\textcolor{var(--text-normal)}{-5}}} & 15 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right] \Big\uparrow\ \text{back substitution}
> $$
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\implies x_2 = -3, \\
> &\hphantom{\implies{}} x_1 = -7 - 3x_2 = -7 + 9 = 2. \\[6pt]
> &\implies b = 2v - 3w. \\
> &\implies b \in \operatorname{span}(v, w).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
