## ST §3.2 The Mean Value Theorem

### ST 3.2.7
**5–8** The graph of a function $f$ is shown. Does $f$ satisfy the hypotheses of the Mean Value Theorem on the interval $[0, 5]$? If so, find a value $c$ that satisfies the conclusion of the Mean Value Theorem on that interval.

### ST 3.2.9
**9–12** Verify that the function satisfies the three hypotheses of Rolle's Theorem on the given interval. Then find all numbers $c$ that satisfy the conclusion of Rolle's Theorem.
$$
f(x) = 2x^2 - 4x + 5, \quad [-1, 3]
$$

### ST 3.2.11
**9–12** Verify that the function satisfies the three hypotheses of Rolle's Theorem on the given interval. Then find all numbers $c$ that satisfy the conclusion of Rolle's Theorem.
$$
f(x) = \sin(x/2), \quad [\pi/2, 3\pi/2]
$$

### ST 3.2.13
Let $f(x) = 1 - x^{2/3}$. Show that $f(-1) = f(1)$ but there is no number $c$ in $(-1, 1)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$. Why does this not contradict Rolle's Theorem?

### ST 3.2.15
**15–18** Verify that the function satisfies the hypotheses of the Mean Value Theorem on the given interval. Then find all numbers $c$ that satisfy the conclusion of the Mean Value Theorem.
$$
f(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 1, \quad [0, 2]
$$

### ST 3.2.17
**15–18** Verify that the function satisfies the hypotheses of the Mean Value Theorem on the given interval. Then find all numbers $c$ that satisfy the conclusion of the Mean Value Theorem.
$$
f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}, \quad [0, 1]
$$

### ST 3.2.21
Let $f(x) = (x - 3)^{-2}$. Show that there is no value of $c$ in $(1, 4)$ such that $f(4) - f(1) = f'(c)(4 - 1)$. Why does this not contradict the Mean Value Theorem?

### ST 3.2.25
Show that the equation $x^3 - 15x + c = 0$ has at most one solution in the interval $[-2, 2]$.

### ST 3.2.29
If $f(1) = 10$ and $f'(x) \ge 2$ for $1 \le x \le 4$, how small can $f(4)$ possibly be?

### ST 3.2.31
Does there exist a function $f$ such that $f(0) = -1$, $f(2) = 4$, and $f'(x) \le 2$ for all $x$?

### ST 3.2.33
Show that $\sin x < x$ if $0 < x < 2\pi$.

### ST 3.2.35
Use the Mean Value Theorem to prove the inequality
$$
\lvert \sin a - \sin b \rvert \le \lvert a - b \rvert \qquad \text{for all } a \text{ and } b
$$
