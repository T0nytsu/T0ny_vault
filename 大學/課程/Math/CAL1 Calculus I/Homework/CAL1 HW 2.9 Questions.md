## ST §2.9 Linear Approximations and Differentials

### ST 2.9.3
**1–4** Find the linearization $L(x)$ of the function at $a$.
$$
f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}, \quad a = 8
$$

### ST 2.9.13
**11–18** Find the differential of the function.
$$
y = \frac{1 + 2u}{1 + 3u}
$$

### ST 2.9.17
**11–18** Find the differential of the function.
$$
y = \sqrt{t - \cos t}
$$

### ST 2.9.21
**19–22** (a) Find the differential $dy$ and (b) evaluate $dy$ for the given values of $x$ and $dx$.
$$
y = \sqrt{3 + x^2}, \quad x = 1, \quad dx = -0.1
$$

### ST 2.9.23
**23–26** Compute $\Delta y$ and $dy$ for the given values of $x$ and $dx = \Delta x$. Then sketch a diagram like Figure 5 showing the line segments with lengths $dx$, $dy$, and $\Delta y$.
$$
y = x^2 - 4x, \quad x = 3, \quad \Delta x = 0.5
$$

### ST 2.9.29
**27–30** Compare the values of $\Delta y$ and $dy$ if $x$ changes from 1 to 1.05. What if $x$ changes from 1 to 1.01? Does the approximation $\Delta y \approx dy$ become better as $\Delta x$ gets smaller?
$$
f(x) = \sqrt{5 - x}
$$

### ST 2.9.31
**31–36** Use a linear approximation (or differentials) to estimate the given number.
$$
(1.999)^4
$$

### ST 2.9.33
**31–36** Use a linear approximation (or differentials) to estimate the given number.
$$
\sqrt[3]{1001}
$$

### ST 2.9.38
**37–38** Explain, in terms of linear approximations or differentials, why the approximation is reasonable.
$$
\sqrt{4.02} \approx 2.005
$$

### ST 2.9.39
The edge of a cube was found to be 30 cm with a possible error in measurement of 0.1 cm. Use differentials to estimate the maximum possible error, relative error, and percentage error in computing (a) the volume of the cube and (b) the surface area of the cube.

### ST 2.9.41
The circumference of a sphere was measured to be 84 cm with a possible error of 0.5 cm.
- (a) Use differentials to estimate the maximum error in the calculated surface area. What is the relative error?
- (b) Use differentials to estimate the maximum error in the calculated volume. What is the relative error?

### ST 2.9.43
- (a) Use differentials to find a formula for the approximate volume of a thin cylindrical shell with height $h$, inner radius $r$, and thickness $\Delta r$.
- (b) What is the error involved in using the formula from part (a)?

### ST 2.9.45
If a current $I$ passes through a resistor with resistance $R$, Ohm's Law states that the voltage drop is $V = RI$. If $V$ is constant and $R$ is measured with a certain error, use differentials to show that the relative error in calculating $I$ is approximately the same (in magnitude) as the relative error in $R$.
